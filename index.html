About This Story

Obi & Bisa Island

A local tale about migration, community, and how a settlement came to be known as Madapolo—the Land of the Crocodile Head.

South of Halmahera, in North Maluku, lies Obi Island. In front of Obi is a small, elongated island called Bisa Island, once used mainly as plantation land by people living around Obi.

LocationObi Island, south of Halmahera, North Maluku
Main PlaceBisa Island, the northern gateway to Obi
2
Chapter One

Obi Island and Bisa Island

To the south of Halmahera Island lies Obi Island, which in the past was under the influence of the Bacan Sultanate. In front of Obi Island is a small, long island known as Bisa Island.

Bisa Island was an important gateway for people entering and leaving Obi from the north. Long ago, it was not permanently inhabited. People from Obi mostly used it as land for planting crops and gardens.

Although quiet, Bisa Island had fertile soil and a strategic location. Its natural surroundings would later attract people who hoped to build a new home there.

3
Chapter Two

The Arrival of the Buton People

On Southeast Sulawesi, there was the Buton Sultanate. It had friendly ties with the Sultanate of Ternate, one of the sultanates of Maluku Kie Raha.

The Buton people were known as skilled sailors who loved traveling across the sea. They often sailed with relatives, wives, and children. Along their journeys, they traded and sometimes established settlements where they stopped.

Because of the good relationship between Buton and Ternate, Buton traders and migrants were welcomed. They were also welcomed when they planned to settle on Obi Mayor Island, south of Bacan.

4
Chapter Three

A New Settlement

After receiving permission from the Sultan of Ternate and the Sultan of Bacan, the Buton migrants began building houses and opening farmland. They were delighted by Obi Mayor Island: the soil was fertile and the scenery was beautiful.

As years passed, the settlement became crowded. Houses stood close together, and the available land felt too small for the growing families. Some residents began thinking about a wider place where their children and grandchildren could live.

5
Chapter Four

The Advice of Pak Imam

The villagers gathered to discuss their plans with Pak Imam, an elder whose advice they trusted. Some suggested moving to Bisa Island, which had mostly been used for gardens.

Pak Imam did not want to move. He calmly accepted their choice and said:

“All right, if that is what you have chosen. I pray you find a place that fulfills your hopes. I am sorry I cannot go with you. I will remain here, ready to advise the villagers.”

The people respected Pak Imam's decision and prepared to search for their new home.

6
Chapter Five

Searching for a New Place

After making preparations, four people set out in two boats to explore Bisa Island. They examined its coastline, soil, water, and whether it would be safe for a village.

They discovered fertile land, clear water, and a strategic position at the northern entrance to Obi. The island seemed suitable for a new settlement.

The four explorers returned and told the villagers what they had found. Their report encouraged the people to move to Bisa Island little by little.

7
Chapter Six

The Birth of Madapolo

The families gradually moved to Bisa Island. They built houses, opened gardens, and started a new life. Over time, their settlement grew into a village.

They named the village Madapolo. In the Ternate language, the name means “crocodile head.” The name was inspired by Bisa Island's shape, which people said looked like a crocodile's head when viewed from a hill on Bacan Island.

Crocodiles were also often seen in the waters around Madapolo. In local belief, they were not merely animals, but guardians or rulers of the sea surrounding the village.
